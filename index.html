Common Furnace Problems Herriman Homeowners Face

Before you book a repair, it helps to know what might be wrong. Here are the most common issues service technicians see in the Herriman area.

Ignition and Flame Sensor Problems

The ignitor is the part that lights the gas. The flame sensor makes sure the flame is actually burning. When either part gets dirty or fails, your furnace will not heat. In Herriman, the dry air and dust can make these parts fail faster. Repair costs for these parts usually range from about $180 to $580, depending on the exact problem.

Blower Motor Trouble

The blower motor pushes warm air through your vents. If you hear strange noises or notice weak airflow, the blower may be failing. A replacement blower motor can cost between $580 and $1,540, depending on the type of motor your system uses. This is a repair that is usually worth doing if your furnace is otherwise in good shape.

Heat Exchanger Concerns

The heat exchanger is a metal part that keeps dangerous gases away from your home’s air. If it cracks, you have a safety issue. Carbon monoxide can leak into your home. This is not a repair you should delay. If a technician finds a cracked heat exchanger, they will likely recommend turning off the furnace until it is fixed or replaced. Some Herriman homes still have heat exchanger warranties in place, which can lower the cost of this repair.

Repair or Replace? How to Decide

If your furnace is old and needs an expensive repair, you may wonder if you should just get a new one. Here is a simple way to think about it.

Repair usually makes sense if:

  • Your furnace is less than 12 years old.
  • The repair is for a minor part, like a sensor or ignitor.
  • This is the first problem you have had in several years.

Replacement may be smarter if:

  • Your furnace is over 15 years old.
  • You have already paid for two or more repairs in the last two years.
  • The repair costs more than one-third of what a new furnace would cost.
  • Your furnace uses old technology that wastes gas.

A new high-efficiency furnace can use 30 to 40 percent less gas than an old model. Over several Utah winters, that savings can add up. But a new furnace is a big purchase. Get a written quote and ask questions before you decide.

Simple Things You Can Check Before Calling for Repair

Before you pay for a service call, try these simple steps. You might solve the problem yourself and save money.

  1. Check your thermostat. Make sure it is set to "Heat" and the temperature is higher than the current room temperature. Replace the batteries if it has them.
  2. Check the filter. A very dirty filter can stop airflow and make the furnace shut off. If it looks gray and clogged, replace it.
  3. Check the breaker. Look at your electrical panel. If the furnace breaker is tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, call a professional.
  4. Check the furnace switch. There is often a light switch near the furnace that looks like a regular light switch. Make sure it is turned on.

If you have tried these steps and the furnace still will not heat, it is time to call a professional.

Safety First: When to Call Right Away

Some furnace problems are dangerous. Call for repair immediately if you notice:

  • A strong gas smell. Leave the house and call your gas company from outside.
  • A yellow or orange flame instead of a steady blue flame.
  • Any sign of carbon monoxide. If your CO detector goes off, get everyone outside and call for help.
  • Loud banging or grinding noises that do not stop.

These are not do-it-yourself problems. They need a trained technician.
